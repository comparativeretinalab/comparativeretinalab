How do tissues exchange oxygen, nutrients and waste products without blood vessels?

We study the comparative physiology and molecular biology of the retina to understand how gas exchange, nutrient supply and waste removal work in a tissue with little or no blood perfusion.

About

We are comparative and evolutionary physiologists working on the mechanisms underlying gas exchange in vertebrates, specifically the compromises between oxygen and nutrient supply, waste removal, acid–base regulation, and neural function. We use the retina as a model tissue: the most metabolically active tissue in the body, yet one that lacks blood vessels, because vessels sitting in the light path would scatter light and blur vision. That trade-off makes the retina an ideal system for testing the general limits of gas and metabolite exchange in tissue. We combine cardiorespiratory and electrophysiology with OMICs tools to trace how oxygen, glucose and metabolic waste move through tissue, from the whole animal down to single cells and mitochondria.
